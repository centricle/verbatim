Speeches, Writings and Founding Documents

The Constitution and each of its amendments, presidential addresses and papers, Supreme Court opinions, speeches in Congress, and other speeches, letters and essays.

A second collection, Voices, holds speeches and essays by Mark Twain and Winston Churchill, two of the speakers quoted in Vs. Each figure counts it apart from Core. Every record is the text as its source published it, with its date, its source and the basis for publishing it here. Search it. Filter it.

Two pages are built from these records: the Amendments to the Constitution in order, and Words Over Time, word counts across the State of the Union and the inaugural addresses.

How the corpus is built
  • Text is as each source published it. Running heads, page numbers, editors’ notes and footnotes are removed. Project Gutenberg texts are reflowed, since their line breaks are that edition’s, not the author’s.
  • Dates are as exact as the source allows: a day, a month or a year. An amendment is dated by the completion of its ratification. Undated records are listed last.
  • Left out: records with no basis for republishing them here, such as tweets, debate transcripts and campaign speeches, and compiled works credited to a single author.
  • Contains Parliamentary information licensed under the Open Parliament Licence v3.0.
