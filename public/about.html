About

Primary source data, made explorable

I wanted to search what the founders actually wrote. Not someone’s interpretation. Not a curated excerpt in a textbook. The actual text, alongside everything that came after, in one index. That was the first project. Then it kept going.

The words

What was said and written, as its source published it. Every record carries its date, its source and the basis for publishing it here.

  • Speeches, Writings and Founding DocumentsThe Constitution and each of its amendments, presidential addresses and papers, Supreme Court opinions, speeches in Congress, and other speeches, letters and essays. Search the full text, or read any record whole.
  • Contents of the CorpusEvery speaker in the corpus under each official roster that lists them, with their records and years, and the State of the Union and inaugural addresses member by member.
  • Amendments to the ConstitutionThe twenty-seven amendments in order, each with its text and the dates it was proposed, ratified and certified.
  • Words Over TimeUp to three words counted in each year’s State of the Union messages or inaugural addresses, per million words.
  • Vs.Curated debates using real quotes. Churchill vs. Twain, Lincoln vs. Jefferson. Two voices, one topic, side by side.

The record

Public data should be publicly explorable. Government agencies publish datasets that are technically available but practically inaccessible: bulk downloads, PDFs, paywalled FOIA responses, formats that require a data pipeline to read. Verbatim takes those datasets and makes them searchable, filterable, and visible.

  • Senate Stock Trades7,740 stock transactions disclosed by U.S. senators under the STOCK Act.
  • COMPAS7,214 criminal defendants, their COMPAS recidivism scores and their outcomes. The data ProPublica published with its analysis of the algorithm.
  • Fatal Police ShootingsThe Washington Post’s Fatal Force database. 10,430 people shot and killed by on-duty police officers since 2015.
  • Campaign Finance3,457 FEC-registered candidates for the 2024 cycle. Who raised what, from where.
  • Qualified Immunity5,500+ federal appellate qualified immunity cases, 2010–2020, as the Institute for Justice collected them.
  • Automated Driving and Driver Assistance CrashesEvery crash report filed with NHTSA under Standing General Order 2021-01 by the makers and operators of vehicles with automated driving systems or Level 2 driver assistance. Narratives and redactions as filed.
  • Executive Orders and ProclamationsThe executive orders and proclamations the Federal Register has published since 1994, each with its number, its dates, its citation and the Federal Register’s notes on which later orders revoked, amended or superseded it. Text as published.
  • Clemency RecipientsEvery pardon and commutation on the Justice Department’s clemency recipient lists, Richard Nixon to the present, with the recipients DOJ names for two group commutations. Names, districts, offenses and sentences as DOJ printed them.

The approach

Everything gets the same treatment: search, filter, visualize. No editorializing, no analysis, no conclusions. The data speaks or it doesn’t. Verbatim provides the microphone, not the script.

Choosing what to index is a judgment, and so is setting two speakers side by side in Vs. The curation is editorial; the words are not.

Every script that fetches, parses and loads these datasets is public, along with the queries behind every search: github.com/centricle/verbatim.

The name is the tell. Not “interpreted.” Not “analyzed.” Not “summarized.” Verbatim.
