Senate Stock Trades

Your Senators’ Stock Trades

The STOCK Act (2012) requires members of Congress to publicly disclose stock trades within 45 days. The Senate publishes these as structured electronic filings. The House publishes PDFs.

This is the Senate data. 7,740 transactions from 53 senators, 2012–2021. Every purchase, every sale, every ticker. Search it. Filter it. See who’s trading what.

Data U.S. Senate electronic filings, via Senate Stock Watcher

Why Senate only?

Same law, two implementations. The Senate files electronically — structured data, machine-readable, downloadable. The House files paper forms as PDFs. To get the same information, you’d need to download thousands of individual PDFs and parse them.

The one project that did this work (House Stock Watcher) went offline. Its data is gone. The Senate’s trading activity is searchable by anyone with a browser. The House’s trading activity is searchable by anyone with a PDF parsing pipeline and cloud infrastructure.

Whether this is incompetence or design is left as an exercise for the reader.

Restrictions on use

The transactions come from financial disclosure reports filed with the Senate and compiled by Senate Stock Watcher through March 2021. Federal law limits how the reports may be used, 5 U.S.C. § 13107(c)(1):

“It shall be unlawful for any person to obtain or use a report— (A) for any unlawful purpose; (B) for any commercial purpose, other than by news and communications media for dissemination to the general public; (C) for determining or establishing the credit rating of any individual; or (D) for use, directly or indirectly, in the solicitation of money for any political, charitable, or other purpose.”
